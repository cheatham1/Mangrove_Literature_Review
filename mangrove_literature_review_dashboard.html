Plastic pollution in mangrove ecosystems: impacts, mitigation actions, and policy responses

213 sources reviewed· 2002–2026· 7 thematic areas· 6 geographic regions
This scoping review synthesised 213 sources spanning 24 years (2002–2026) across peer-reviewed literature, grey literature, policy documents, and intergovernmental reports. The evidence base has grown rapidly: 55% of all sources were published from 2023 onward, reflecting the emergence of mangrove plastic pollution as a distinct research field.

Evidence by thematic area
Volume of evidence varies significantly across research themes. Plastic pollution state assessments have the strongest evidence base; mitigation practices and transboundary dynamics have the least.
Growth of the evidence base
Research output has accelerated sharply since 2023, driven by the Global Plastics Treaty negotiations and growing recognition of mangroves as pollutant sinks. Mitigation research is the most recent theme — 85% published since 2023.
Geographic distribution of evidence
Primary field research concentrates in Asia and the Middle East, while policy and governance studies are predominantly global in scope.
Regional evidence profile
Asia / Middle East contributes the largest body of primary research (23%), particularly on plastic pollution state and microplastics in sediments — reflecting Southeast Asian fieldwork concentration.

Global studies (55%) are mainly policy analyses, intergovernmental reports, and ecosystem reviews — important for framing but limited in site-specific data.

Africa (8%) has emerging evidence, mostly from East Africa. West African mangrove systems are notably underrepresented.

Assessing the quality and composition of the evidence base is essential for understanding how robust the review's findings are. This section examines literature types, quality scoring, and the intersection of research themes with geographic coverage.
Source types across thematic areas
Peer-reviewed evidence is strongest in plastic pollution research (83%) and transboundary research (80%), but weakest in policy analysis (22%), which relies heavily on intergovernmental and legal sources.
Quality score distribution
Sources were scored on focus relevance (0–10), literature type (3–5), keyword match (0–3), and geographic specificity (0–3), giving a maximum of 21. The bimodal distribution reflects two evidence tiers.
Quality bands
38% of scored sources fell in the highest band. No sources scored below 52%, indicating a well-curated corpus with strong minimum relevance.
